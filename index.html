Who we are

Brilliant Nodes is run by a team with more than 20 years of experience in financial markets and technology: trading, investment banking and fund management, digital-asset funds across arbitrage, multi-strategy and early-stage venture, and doctoral research on artificial neural networks.

We set up Brilliant Nodes to work on the infrastructure side of new networks: the nodes, validators and apps they run on.

We run our infrastructure the way regulated finance runs its operations: separated keys, tested and pinned releases, written procedures, and a check on every result.

What we do

We run our own servers on six continents. That spread gives a network the geographic diversity it needs, and keeps it stable if one region has trouble.

Launch-day capacity
We bring rented machines online within hours of a launch, across several providers. We have run more than 100 at once, in more than 20 countries on four continents.
Seed and hub nodes
Permanent seed and hub servers in Europe, North America and Asia, plus our own stratum hubs. Our machines and outside users sync from them, and spreading them across continents keeps a network reachable and stable if one region has trouble.
Validator operations
We take part in the validator programmes of GenLayer, Flop Network and gno.land, with separate owner and operator keys, full monitoring and servers spread across continents.
Mining optimisation
Every machine is calibrated. We compare its reported output with what it really delivers, and tune or replace what falls short.
Apps on chain
We build and run decentralised apps on GenLayer, each with public code, a live demo and a written explanation.
GPU and AI compute
The same fleet and tooling run GPU work beyond mining, such as AI inference for useful-work networks.
Watchdogs
Every node and worker has its own watchdog. A hung process is restarted, and we get an alert.
Security
Pinned, hash-checked binaries only. Owner and operator keys are kept apart, and recovery seeds are stored offline. How we keep things secure
Reward checks
Every hour we check that rewards reached the wallet they were meant for.

How we prepare a launch

The first hours of a new network matter most, so we prepare in fixed steps and check every one.

  1. The day before

    A full dress rehearsal: every step run end to end on test machines.

  2. 3 hours before

    Our hub and seed nodes are up in three regions, so no machine has to wait for a chain download.

  3. 2 hours before

    Machines rented across several providers, with spares. Every one is set up, checked and ready 30 minutes before the start.

  4. The last 3 hours

    We check the project’s releases every five minutes and apply any change at once.

  5. Launch

    Our machines start working from the first second.

  6. 10 minutes after

    The first check that rewards reach the wallets they should. Then the same check every hour.

Recent development projects: GenLayer

GenLayer is a blockchain whose validators can read public web pages and use AI models to agree on what those pages say. We use it to settle agreements against public records: money waits in escrow, and moves only when the validators agree. Every app below has public code, a live demo and a written article, and uses fictional companies and test tokens only.

Four of our builds have been accepted in GenLayer’s builder programme so far; the rest are under review.

Launch Claim Checker

$14bn+ taken in by crypto scams in 2025 (Chainalysis, via Decrypt)

Checks a new coin’s launch claims, such as fair mining or no premine, against what its block explorer shows, and records the verdict on chain. Its claim bounties hold test tokens behind a claim, paid to the first person who shows the claim is false.

ClaimEscrow

$25bn litigation finance, the market claim trading belongs to, 2025 estimate (Custom Market Insights)

A safer way to buy and sell bankruptcy claims. The price waits in escrow while validators read the claim’s entry on the public claims register; the seller is paid for the amount finally allowed, and the buyer gets the rest back.

PrivateCreditGate

$1.7–3trn private credit, published estimates for 2025–26 (Universal Asset Owners, S&P Global)

Checks the financial limits a borrower has promised to keep (loan covenants) straight from its published report, instead of someone retyping figures from a PDF. Validators read the report, code applies the loan terms, and breach, cure and repayment are settled on chain.

ReportReader, RuleCheck and MeterReader

Three building blocks that other contracts can use. ReportReader reads exact figures from published company reports. RuleCheck checks facts on public pages against rules written as data. MeterReader turns a public statistics table into one agreed reading.
