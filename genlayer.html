Apps and building blocks on GenLayer that settle agreements against public records. Four have been accepted in GenLayer’s builder programme so far.

What GenLayer does

GenLayer is a blockchain whose validators can read public web pages and use AI models to agree on what those pages say. We use it to settle agreements against public records: money waits in escrow, and moves only when the validators agree.

  1. Money waits. The buyer’s payment is locked in the contract.
  2. Validators read. Several validators each read the same public page.
  3. They must agree. The result counts only when they agree on what the page says.
  4. Money moves. The contract pays out by that result, with no one in the middle.

Every app below has public code, a live demo and a written article. All of them use fictional companies and test tokens only.

Launch Claim Checker · accepted

Checks a new coin’s launch claims, such as fair mining or no premine, against what its block explorer shows, and records the verdict on chain. Seven test cases ran live on GenLayer’s public test network: 41 transactions, all matching our record.

ClaimEscrow

A safer way to buy and sell bankruptcy claims. The price waits in escrow while validators read the claim’s entry on the public claims register; the seller is paid for the amount finally allowed, and the buyer gets the rest back. Nine fictional deals ran on GenLayer’s public test network: 70 transactions, all finalized and matching our record.

Open ClaimEscrow · Article · Code · Video · Contract

PrivateCreditGate

Checks the financial limits a borrower has promised to keep (loan covenants) straight from its published report, instead of someone retyping figures from a PDF. Validators read the report, code applies the loan terms, and breach, cure and repayment are settled on chain. 63 transactions on GenLayer’s public test network, each checked against the chain.

Open PrivateCreditGate · Article · Code · Video · Contract

RigSettle

Escrow for renting mining power. The buyer’s payment is locked, validators read the buyer’s mining pool page, and the escrow is split by the hashrate actually delivered. 11 of 11 test cases passed live on GenLayer Studio, and the contract also runs on the public test network.

Open RigSettle · Article · Code · Video · Contract

ReportReader, RuleCheck and MeterReader · all three accepted

Three building blocks that other contracts can use. ReportReader reads exact figures from published company reports. RuleCheck checks facts on public pages against rules written as data. MeterReader turns a public statistics table into one agreed reading. ReportReader ran 35 live transactions, all finalized; MeterReader’s readings agreed exactly on the public test network; RuleCheck ran four live checks, with three validators agreeing on each.

Research behind the apps

Each app starts from a real problem, written up in plain words: how it is handled today, where that falls short, what we propose, and what our tests showed.

  • Who carries the risk when a bankruptcy claim is sold?
  • The weakest link in private credit is a person typing numbers
  • Paying for rented mining power without a judge
  • “No premine”, “fair launch”: who checks the promises new coins make?
